Algorithm Test Bed — Project Summary
=====================================
(~7 weeks, Apr 19 → Jun 5 2026, ~60 commits)

WHAT YOU BUILT
--------------
A Rust simulation engine (sim/) with a Bevy viewer (viewer/) and a Python RL
training pipeline (rl/), bridged by PyO3. It started as a playground for classic
pathfinding and ended as a focused reinforcement-learning project: a single agent
learning to collect gold and deposit it at base, trying to beat scripted baselines.

THE ARC — WHY EACH PHASE HAPPENED
---------------------------------
1. "Let me see the algorithms run" (Apr 19 – May 2).
   Began with the concrete and visual: A*, then D* Lite, a HUD, camera controls,
   debug overlays. The instinct was sound — build the thing you can watch. Even
   here the pull toward general structure over one-offs showed up (a "universal
   debug drawing interface" replacing per-algorithm hacks).

2. "Make it a game" (May 14 – 17).
   Items, pickups, combat, teams, FSM / Behavior Tree / GOAP strategies, a
   scoreboard, a match-end screen. Ambition expanded fast — multi-agent combat
   with several competing AI paradigms.

3. "Make it learn" (May 17 – 21).
   PyO3 bindings, headless mode, an RL pipeline, Hydra config, a Rich dashboard.
   The pivot from scripted AI to learned AI. The project's real center of gravity
   moved here.

4. The Great Simplification (May 23 – Jun 5).
   The most telling stretch. The commit verbs: Remove. Remove. Remove. Deleted
   combat, enemies, the FSM, the behavior trees, A* and D* Lite as agent
   strategies, whole config stages. Scoped down to single-agent gold rush only,
   and kept the combat engine dormant rather than deleted — parked as the
   foundation for a deliberately separate future project. The late work (PBRS,
   then removing it; intra-option discounting; SMDP; a trace recorder) is the
   fine, patient tuning of one well-defined problem.

THE OUTCOME
-----------
A clean, intentionally-scoped system: a refactored Rust engine
(world/entity/engine/rl/algorithm), MaskablePPO with temporally-extended A*
navigation options, a reward that's just tick + pickup + deposit + wall-hit, and
tooling (telemetry, plotting, an offline trace recorder) to actually debug why the
agent does what it does. Not a sprawling combat sim — something you can train,
reason about, and explain. The grandeur converted into clarity.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT YOU, AS A PERSON
-------------------------------------
- You learn by making things visible. Every phase added a way to see the system —
  overlays, HUD, scoreboard, dashboards, a trace recorder. You don't trust what
  you can't observe. That's a real strength.

- Your hardest, most mature work was subtraction. Anyone can add features when
  excited. Spending two weeks deleting your own combat system, behavior trees, and
  pathfinding algorithms — code you clearly enjoyed building — to protect one
  trainable problem is discipline most people don't have. You learned that scope
  is a feature.

- You stopped treating "delete" as "fail." Keeping the combat engine dormant for a
  future, separately-scoped game is the move of someone who's made peace with
  deferring ambition instead of cramming it in. You pushed back on your own
  premature complexity (the LSTM justified only by hypothetical future self-play,
  the over-broad reward/obs space — "too much to train and learn for my first
  agent").

- You're willing to be a beginner on purpose. Calling this "my first agent" and
  choosing the simplest version is humility in service of actually finishing. The
  whole back half of this project is you learning that the goal isn't to build the
  most impressive thing — it's to build the thing you can finish, understand, and
  stand on next.

ONE SENTENCE TO KEEP
--------------------
You started chasing breadth and ended having learned the value of depth — that
finishing a small, well-understood problem teaches you more than half-building a
large one.

The combat game is still waiting in the engine, intact. When you build it, you'll
be building it because you finished this — not instead of finishing this. That's
the difference.
